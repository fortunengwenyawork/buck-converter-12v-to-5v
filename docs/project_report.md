12V to 5V Buck Converter Design, Simulation, and PCB Implementation

Abstract

This project presents the design, simulation, and PCB implementation of a 12V to 5V DC-DC buck converter using the LM2596S-5 switching regulator. The objective was to develop a practical understanding of switching power supplies while gaining experience in LTspice simulation, PCB design using KiCad, and manufacturing file generation. The converter was designed to provide a regulated 5V output from a 12V DC input while supporting loads up to approximately 2A. Simulation results were used to verify converter operation prior to PCB implementation. The final design was transferred to KiCad, routed, verified using Design Rule Checking (DRC), and exported as Gerber manufacturing files.

Introduction

Linear voltage regulators dissipate excess voltage as heat and become inefficient when large voltage drops are required. Switching regulators overcome this limitation by storing and transferring energy through inductors and capacitors, allowing significantly higher efficiency.

The buck converter is one of the most common switching regulator topologies. Its purpose is to convert a higher DC voltage into a lower regulated DC voltage. In this project, a buck converter was designed to convert a 12V input into a regulated 5V output suitable for powering embedded systems, microcontrollers, sensors, and other low-voltage electronics.

Design Objectives

The primary goals of the project were:

* Design a 12V to 5V switching regulator.
* Simulate converter behavior using LTspice.
* Analyze startup response and ripple performance.
* Design a PCB layout using KiCad.
* Verify PCB integrity using Design Rule Checking.
* Generate manufacturing Gerber files.
* Document the complete engineering workflow.

Theoretical Analysis

The duty cycle of an ideal buck converter is given by:

D = Vout / Vin

Substituting the design values:

D = 5 / 12

D = 0.417

Therefore, the switch is expected to remain ON approximately 41.7% of each switching cycle.

The output power is:

Pout = Vout × Iout

Pout = 5 × 2

Pout = 10 W

The equivalent load resistance is:

Rload = Vout / Iout

Rload = 5 / 2

Rload = 2.5 Ω

These calculations establish the expected operating conditions prior to simulation.

LTspice Simulation

The converter was modeled in LTspice using the LM2596 switching regulator topology.

Several simulation studies were performed:

Startup Response

A transient analysis was conducted to observe the converter startup behavior. The output voltage rose from zero and settled near the desired 5V operating point. This confirmed stable startup operation.

Output Voltage Ripple

The output capacitor filters switching noise generated by the regulator. The simulation demonstrated a small periodic ripple superimposed on the 5V output voltage, confirming proper filtering performance.

Inductor Current Ripple

The inductor current waveform exhibited the expected triangular shape characteristic of continuous-conduction-mode buck converters. Ripple magnitude remained within acceptable limits.

Inductor Startup Current

The startup current waveform demonstrated how energy builds in the magnetic field of the inductor during converter startup. The current increased smoothly before reaching steady-state operation.

Switch Node Voltage

The switch node waveform alternated between high and low voltage states corresponding to the regulator switching action. This verified correct operation of the energy transfer mechanism.

Simulation results confirmed that the converter behaved as expected under nominal operating conditions.

PCB Design

After simulation validation, the design was implemented in KiCad.

The schematic was recreated and footprints were assigned to all components. Special attention was given to routing high-current paths between the regulator, diode, inductor, and capacitors.

Wide copper traces were used in power paths to reduce resistive losses. A ground plane was added to improve current return paths and reduce noise susceptibility.

The final PCB consists of:

* Input connector
* LM2596S-5 regulator
* SS34 Schottky diode
* 68 µH inductor
* Input filter capacitor
* Output filter capacitor
* Output connector

The completed layout was checked using KiCad’s Design Rule Checker.

No critical design violations were reported.

Results

The project successfully demonstrated the complete workflow required to design a switching power supply from concept to manufacturing output.

The converter achieved the desired 12V-to-5V conversion objective through simulation and PCB implementation. LTspice simulations verified correct electrical behavior, while KiCad was used to create a manufacturable PCB layout. The final design passed Design Rule Checking and Gerber files were successfully produced.

Conclusion

This project provided practical experience in power electronics, circuit simulation, PCB design, and engineering documentation. Through the design of a 12V to 5V buck converter, the complete engineering process was explored, beginning with theoretical analysis and ending with fabrication-ready manufacturing files.

Future work may include fabrication of the PCB, efficiency measurements, thermal analysis, EMI evaluation, and optimization of the converter for higher current applications.
